acting as

Two identities in this browser, both real — separate keys, separate MLS leaves, separate snapshots. Switching is not a filter on a view; it is being somebody else. Join a room as each, and watch what the first has to do to keep reading once the second arrives.

This key lives in this browser and nowhere else. Clearing site data destroys it, and with it every room you joined — nobody can re-issue it for you. That is the point, and it is also the risk.

Or join one this site has never heard of. Paste a room's DID: if it is a did:peer:2 it carries the mediator its owner listens on, and everything else follows from that one lookup — no host URL, no catalogue, no configuration.

3 · How you got in

Admission is a two-party act, and both halves are shown. The owner decides and issues; this browser checks before it acts on anything. Neither side's checks substitute for the other's — the owner's stop a forged invitation adding a member, and yours stop your own key holder being filled with a room you never agreed to join.

    Two numbers, because they are different repairs. Behind the room's epoch means a commit has not been delivered. Earliest equal to current means the key chain has not arrived — you can read from where you joined, not before.

    4 · Records

    What is real here, and what is not

    Real. Your key, minted and held in WebAssembly — this page never sees the private half. The MLS group, the sealing and the epoch chain are vti-rooms itself, the same crate the services link. The invitation, the membership and the authority are DTG credentials the room signed. Every record operation goes to the real room-host as a signed Trust Task, and it authorises each one against a chain narrowed to that single action — read to list, write to store. It stores ciphertext and has no code path that could read it.

    And none of it needs a URL. A room says which mediator its owner listens on, and this page reaches both that owner and the room's host through it — over TSP where they offer it, DIDComm where they do not. So the site works against a room and a host it has never seen, neither of which has to be somewhere your browser is allowed to call. What that buys is reach, not authority: the host still takes who you are from the signature on each request and what you may do from the chain the room issued, so the same bytes would be exactly as authorised had they gone over HTTP.

    You hold two keys, and they do different jobs. The one above is your room identity: it lives in wasm, signs everything, and is what the room's credentials name. The other is a throwaway address the mediator delivers to, minted fresh each session. Every request carries a proof of each, and names the second inside the first — so a signed request cannot be picked up and replayed by somebody else's connection.

    Try this first. Join a room as alice, write something, then switch to bob and join too. Bob reads what Alice wrote before he arrived — not because the host handed him anything readable, but because he walked the epoch key chain: each rung seals one epoch's key under its successor's, and a holder of the current key walks backwards down them. Watch earliest readable go to 1 the moment the rungs arrive.

    Backwards only, and that is the property rather than a limit: a rung lets you go down from a key you hold, never up to one you do not — which is what makes a removal forward-only. Somebody removed at epoch 7 cannot climb to 8.

    Then this. Retract a record in each room. The Workshop grants curate and the Library does not, so the same button works in one and is refused in the other — refused in this tab, before any request is made, because attenuate will not widen what the room gave you. "You were never given this" and "the host disagreed" are different sentences, and only the first can be answered by asking the owner.

    Standing in. The owner. In a real room that is a person with an agent, deciding; here it admits anyone who asks and says so. What it issues is not pretended — the credentials are real and the ceremony is the same either side of the decision.

    Deliberately absent. The private tier, whose subject binding needs a zero-knowledge profile the working group has not settled — a demo tier that quietly behaved like this one would misrepresent it.
